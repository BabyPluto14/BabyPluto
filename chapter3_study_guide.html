The accounting cycle

Every topic below is just one box in this flow. Memorise the shape.

OPENING BALANCE SHEET → open accounts in journal + ledger │ RECORD ALL TRANSACTIONS during the year (journal + ledger) │ 1st PROVISIONAL TRIAL BALANCE ← check: does debit = credit? │ INVENTORY → END-OF-PERIOD ENTRIES [mostly later chapters] │ 2nd PROVISIONAL TRIAL BALANCE → result BEFORE tax │ TAXES → FINAL TRIAL BALANCE │ FINANCIAL STATEMENTS = Balance sheet + Income statement + Notes │ DISTRIBUTION OF PROFIT after tax
Where we areThis chapter mostly lives in the top half (open → record → trial balance → result). Inventory adjustments and tax come in later chapters, so the profit we compute here is before end-of-period adjustments and tax.
02

The balance sheet

A snapshot — a photo — of the company's financial position at one moment in time.

ASSETS = EQUITY + LIABILITIES

Two sides that are always equal. The left shows the operating resources the company uses (the "use of funds"); the right shows the financing resources — where the money came from (the "origin of funds"). Every euro of resource had to be financed by someone: the owner (equity) or a third party the company owes (a liability). That's why the sides must match. If they don't, you made an error.

The sandwich-shop intuition Opening a shop, you need a building, counter, oven, van (long-lasting → fixed assets) and bread stock, bank, cash (fast-moving → current assets). That's the assets side. To pay for it: your own savings (equity), a bank loan, and suppliers letting you pay later (both liabilities). Same total, two viewpoints.

Ordering rules in our template. Assets are listed by increasing liquidity (fixed assets first, then inventory → receivables → bank → cash last). Equity + liabilities by increasing callability (equity first — never called; then payables >1yr; then payables ≤1yr).

The balance sheet always stays balanced After every transaction: Δ Assets = Δ Equity + Δ Liabilities. There are only four possible moves — this is your #1 self-check:
#MoveExample
①Asset ↑ & (E+L) ↑Buy furniture on credit
②Asset ↓ & (E+L) ↓Pay a supplier from the bank
③Asset ↑ & another asset ↓Customer pays: bank ↑, receivable ↓
④(E+L) ↑ & another (E+L) ↓Short-term debt becomes a long-term loan
03

Debit & credit — the heart of everything

Each balance sheet item gets its own T-account: debit = left, credit = right. All accounts together = the general ledger.

Notation warningThe slides sometimes print the T-account header as "C … D". Ignore the letter order — always draw debit on the LEFT, credit on the RIGHT.

Learn this table cold. Rule of thumb: an account "lives" on the same side it appears in the balance sheet.

Account typeIncreaseDecreaseHome / normal balance
Asset (left of B/S)DebitCreditDebit
Liability / Equity (right of B/S)CreditDebitCredit
Expense (class 6)DebitCreditDebit
Revenue (class 7)CreditDebitCredit
The iron law — for every entry: total DEBIT = total CREDIT

This is double-entry bookkeeping. Every transaction hits at least two accounts and the debits equal the credits. Assets & Expenses are debit-natured; Liabilities, Equity & Revenues are credit-natured. (Deeper why: an expense reduces equity, a revenue increases it — so they point the same way as assets and equity respectively.)

The 4-question procedure — say it out loud every time ① Which document?   ② Which account — asset/liability/equity or expense/revenue?   ③ Increase or decrease?   ④ Debit or credit?
04

The income statement

The balance sheet is a photo; the income statement is the film — did we make money over the period?

Revenues − Expenses = Result (Profit or Loss)
Operating revenues − Operating expenses = OPERATING PROFIT (LOSS) + Financial revenues − Financial expenses = PROFIT (LOSS) BEFORE TAXES − Income taxes = PROFIT AVAILABLE FOR APPROPRIATION

Revenues (class 7) and expenses (class 6) split into operating vs financial, each into recurring vs non-recurring. Examples: recurring operating revenue = sales of goods for resale; recurring operating expense = purchases, electricity, wages; recurring financial expense = interest on loans, bank charges.

05

VAT — the one everyone trips over

The company is a middleman that collects VAT for the government. VAT is not the company's income or expense — it flows through the company.

On a PURCHASE → an ASSET You pay VAT to your supplier. The tax office will refund it later, so you have a claim / receivable.

"VAT to reclaim" (411) = ASSET → increases on the debit side.
On a SALE → a LIABILITY Your customer pays VAT to you. You must hand it to the tax office, so you have a debt.

"VAT due" (451) = LIABILITY → increases on the credit side.
Memorise the two skeletonsGross = Net × 1.21  (VAT = Net × 0.21). The supplier/customer line always carries the gross; the tax office gets the VAT slice.

07

The general ledger & the journal

General ledgerThe book of all T-accounts, organised by account. Left page = debit amounts, right page = credit amounts. Answers: "what is the situation of this one account right now?"
JournalThe accounting diary — all transactions entered without delay, faithfully, completely, in time order. Organised by date. Legally required. Answers: "what happened, chronologically?"

Every journal entry has the same standardised form: date · order number · account numbers · the entry (debited accounts, then "@ /at" credited accounts + a short description) · debit column · credit column. Debited accounts are written first; credited accounts are indented under "@".

08

The trial balance

An interim control balance (not required by law) listing all accounts to check the books are internally consistent.

With footings (totals)For each account, total the debit side and the credit side. Check: total debit-totals = total credit-totals.
With balancesFor each account, take the difference (debit − credit) as one balance. Check: total debit balances = total credit balances. This is closing the accounts.
It is not foolproofThe trial balance proves the arithmetic is consistent — it will not catch an entry posted to the wrong account, one omitted entirely, or one recorded twice, because those keep debit = credit.
09

From opening to closing — where the result comes from

Once all accounts are closed, the profit shows up in two independent places that must agree.

Σ revenue balances (cl.7) 10 200,00 − Σ expense balances (cl.6) −7 818,75 = PROFIT 2 381,25
Σ asset balances 140 784,25 − Σ (equity + liab.) bal. −138 403,00 = PROFIT 2 381,25
The link in one sentenceThe income statement explains why equity changed during the year; the profit it computes is exactly what makes the closing balance sheet balance again.

Appropriation. A profit (69-group) can be added to reserves, carried forward, or distributed to partners. A loss (79-group) can be withdrawn from equity, carried forward, or covered by partners. Example — carry the whole profit forward:

693Profit to be carried forward2 381,25
@ 140Profit carried forward (equity)2 381,25

13

Common mistakes

❌ Mistake✅ Fix / why
Treating loan repayment as an expenseOnly the interest is an expense; principal just reduces the liability.
Putting VAT to reclaim on the credit sideIt's an asset (a receivable) → debit. VAT due is the liability (credit).
Booking the gross amount as the expense/assetThe expense/asset is the net; VAT is booked separately; only the supplier/customer line carries gross.
Forgetting debit = credit in every entryIf they don't match you're missing a line or used the wrong side.
Recording stock cost at the moment of saleHere, purchases are expensed and sales booked as revenue; cost-matching is an end-of-period entry (later chapter).
Confusing 411 VAT to reclaim with 451 VAT dueReclaim = paid on a purchase (asset). Due = collected on a sale (liability).
Wrong ordering of assets/liabilitiesAssets by increasing liquidity (cash last); E+L by increasing callability (equity first).
Thinking the trial balance proves correctnessIt only proves debit = credit. Wrong-account, omitted, duplicated entries slip through.
Booking a mixed private/business cost fully as company expenseSplit it: only the business % is an expense; private % → receivable on manager (416); VAT reclaimable only on the business part.

  • VAT: purchase → 411 VAT to reclaim (asset, debit); sale → 451 VAT due (liability, credit). Gross = Net × 1.21.
