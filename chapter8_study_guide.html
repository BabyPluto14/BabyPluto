01

The core mechanism

The whole chapter hangs on one idea: the inventory account only moves at year-end.

  • During the year: every purchase → expense account 604; every sale → revenue account 70. The inventory account 340 is left untouched — it keeps its opening balance all year.
  • Only at year-end (an end-of-period entry, after a physical stock-take) do we book the change in inventories, so 340 shows the correct closing value (asset) and the income statement shows the correct cost of goods sold.
COGS = Opening inventory + Purchases − Closing inventory  ·  Change = Closing − Opening
The one journal entry (stock increased)Closing > opening means stock went up:
31/12
340Inventory of GFR – Cost of acquisition[change]
@ 6094Changes in inventories of GFR[change]
340 is an asset → debit to increase. 6094 (an expense account) is credited — more stock REDUCES net cost of goods sold (line 609). If stock DECREASED, flip the entry.
02

Cost of acquisition vs market value

Two values compete for every item; you always compare them (see §8).

ValueDefinition
Cost of acquisitionPurchase cost + other/additional costs (transport…), excluding refundable taxes (VAT). Estimated via specific identification, FIFO, LIFO, or weighted average.
Market value
(net realisable value)		Estimated selling price − estimated costs of completion − estimated costs of sale.

The lower of the two always prevails — a consequence of the prudence principle.

04

FIFO — First In, First Out

Goods bought first are sold first → the goods left in stock come from the most recent purchases.

Value the 80 closing units from the newest purchase: 80 × 20,00 (05/09) = €1 600. Change = 1 600 − 1 000 = +600.

340Inventory of GFR – Cost of acquisition600,00
@ 6094Changes in inventories of GFR600,00

Closing inventory (BS) = 1 600 · COGS = 1 000 + 3 200 − 1 600 = 2 600 · Operating profit = 4 140 − 2 600 = 1 540.

IntuitionIn rising prices FIFO leaves the newest, dearest units in stock → highest closing inventory, lowest COGS, highest profit.
05

LIFO — Last In, First Out

Goods bought last are sold first → the goods left in stock come from the earliest purchases.

Work step by stepCheck what's in stock at each sale — you can't just grab the oldest layers at the end, because a layer may already be sold.
After…Stock layers
Opening100 @ 10
Sale 60 (15/02)sell 60 @ 10 → 40 @ 10
Purchase 80 @ 1540 @ 10 + 80 @ 15
Sale 60 (23/08)sell 60 @ 15 → 40 @ 10 + 20 @ 15
Purchase 100 @ 2040 @ 10 + 20 @ 15 + 100 @ 20
Sale 80 (21/12)sell 80 @ 20 → 40 @ 10 + 20 @ 15 + 20 @ 20

Closing = 40×10 + 20×15 + 20×20 = €1 100. Change = +100. Entry: 340 D 100 / 6094 C 100.

Closing inventory (BS) = 1 100 · COGS = 3 100 · Operating profit = 1 040.

IntuitionIn rising prices LIFO leaves the oldest, cheapest units in stock → lowest closing inventory, highest COGS, lowest profit.
06

Weighted average price

Value the closing units at an average purchase price — two flavours.

Overall (periodic) average

Average over opening + all purchases: (1 000 + 3 200) / (100 + 180) = 4 200 / 280 = €15,00/unit. Closing = 80 × 15 = €1 200. Change = +200 → COGS 3 000, profit 1 140.

Moving average — recompute after each purchase

New average after a purchase(old value + purchase value) ÷ (old units + purchased units). E.g. after 29/03: (400 + 1 200) ÷ (40 + 80) = 1 600 ÷ 120 = 13,33.

Comparing the methods

Same transactions, four different profits — this is the punchline of the chapter.

Two rulesChoose a method and apply it consistently; the chosen method need not match the physical flow of goods. In rising prices: FIFO → highest profit, LIFO → lowest, averages in between.
08

The market test

Lower-of-cost-or-market: if market value falls below cost, value inventory at market. Prudence — only downward.

OMEGA KAPPA, FIFO chosen. FIFO cost of closing = 1 600. But year-end market price is only 14,00/unit → 80 × 14 = 1 120. Market < cost → use 1 120. Change = 1 120 − 1 000 = +120 (not +600).

31/12
340Inventory of GFR – Cost of acquisition120,00
@ 6094Changes in inventories of GFR120,00
No special account — you simply record the change using the LOWER closing value.

Closing (BS) = 1 120 · COGS = 1 000 + 3 200 − 1 120 = 3 080.

09

Write-offs on inventory

A further reduction for obsolescence, deterioration or an expected decline — using a separate contra-asset account so gross cost stays visible.

Value ↓349 Inventory of GFR – Amounts written off (−) — a contra-asset (credit).
Expense ↑6310 Amounts written off inventories – Increase — an expense (debit).

VICTORIA plc (WAP): opening 150 000; closing at cost 200 000; market 185 000; further decline to 160 000 expected.

Step 1 — change in inventory (market test → 185 000): change = 185 000 − 150 000 = +35 000.

340Inventory of GFR – Cost of acquisition35 000,00
@ 6094Changes in inventories of GFR35 000,00

Step 2 — write-off for the expected drop to 160 000: 185 000 − 160 000 = 25 000.

6310Amounts written off inventories – Increase25 000,00
@ 349Inventory of GFR – Amounts written off (−)25 000,00
Market test vs write-offThe market test sets the value on 340 to the lower of cost/market. A write-off is an extra prudence reduction parked in 349, keeping the gross 340 figure intact. Net book value here = 185 000 − 25 000 = 160 000.

Common mistakes

❌ Mistake✅ Fix / why
Touching 340 during the year on each sale/purchase340 moves only at year-end; purchases → 604, sales → 70 all year.
LIFO grabbed straight from the end without layersTrace stock at every sale — an early layer may already be gone.
Valuing closing stock at the selling priceInventory is valued at cost of acquisition (or lower market), never sales price.
Forgetting the market testCompare closing-at-cost vs market; the lower prevails (prudence).
Confusing market test and write-offMarket test → value on 340 at lower of cost/market. Write-off → extra reduction via 6310/349.
Booking the change on the wrong sideStock up → 340 D / 6094 C. Stock down → 6094 D / 340 C.
Thinking the method must match the real flow of goodsIt need not — only consistency is required.
Mixing up overall and moving WAPOverall = one average for the period; moving = new average after each purchase.
Writing an asset up when market > costNot allowed — lower-of-cost-or-market only works downward.
15

Summary sheet

COGS = Opening + Purchases − Closing  ·  Change = Closing − Opening

Mechanism: all year — purchases → 604, sales → 70, 340 untouched. At year-end book the change: stock up → 340 D / 6094 C (stock ↑ reduces net expense, line 609).

MethodClosing units valued from…
FIFOmost recent purchases
LIFOearliest purchases (step by step!)
Overall WAP(opening + all purchases value) ÷ total units
Moving WAPaverage recomputed after each purchase
  • Rising prices: FIFO → highest profit & closing inventory; LIFO → lowest; averages between. Choose one, apply consistently; needn't match physical flow.
  • Market test: lower of cost or market (NRV = selling price − completion − selling costs). Only downward.
  • Write-off: obsolescence/decline → 6310 (expense, D) / 349 (contra-asset, C).
  • Manufactured: direct vs full costing. Contracts in progress: % of completion vs completed contract.
