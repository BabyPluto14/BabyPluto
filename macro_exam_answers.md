### Question 1.1 – Initial Long-Run Equilibrium (Time 0)

**Diagram description:**

- **Vertical axis:** Price Level (P)
- **Horizontal axis:** Real Output (Y)
- **LRAS:** Vertical line at natural rate of output Y*
- **SRAS₀:** Upward-sloping curve
- **AD₀:** Downward-sloping curve
- All three curves intersect at a single point **(P₀, Y*)**, labelled **Time 0**

```
P
|        LRAS
|          |
|    SRAS₀ |
|   /      |
P₀ +-------+---- (Time 0 equilibrium)
|          |\
|          | AD₀
|          |
+----------+--------> Y
           Y*
```

In long-run equilibrium: actual prices = expected prices, unemployment = natural rate, output = natural rate (Y*).

### Question 1.2 – Short-Run Impact of Power Station Breakdown (Time 1)

**What happens:**
A power station breakdown is an **adverse supply shock**. Electricity is a key input into production. Higher electricity prices raise production costs for firms throughout the economy.

**Short-run effects:**
- Firms face higher production costs → they reduce output and raise prices.
- The **SRAS curve shifts LEFT/UPWARD** (SRAS₀ → SRAS₁).
- The **LRAS also shifts LEFT** (permanent reduction in productive capacity: Y* → Y*').
- AD remains unchanged (no demand-side shock at this point).

**Diagram (add to 1.1):**
- SRAS₁ is to the left/above SRAS₀
- LRAS shifts left to Y*'
- New short-run equilibrium **Time 1**: intersection of AD₀ and SRAS₁
  - Output falls: **Y₁ < Y***
  - Price level rises: **P₁ > P₀**

This situation is called **stagflation**: simultaneously falling output and rising prices.

```
P
|       LRAS' LRAS
|         |    |
|  SRAS₁  |    |
|  SRAS₀  |    |
P₁ +------+    |  (Time 1)
P₀ +------|----|--  (Time 0)
|         |\   |\ AD₀
+----------+---+----> Y
          Y₁  Y*
```

### Question 1.4 – Short-Run Impact on the Phillips Curve (Time 0 → Time 1)

**Diagram description:**

- **Vertical axis:** Inflation rate (π)
- **Horizontal axis:** Unemployment rate (u)
- **LRPC:** Vertical line at natural unemployment rate u*
  - Because the breakdown is permanent, the natural rate of unemployment INCREASES → LRPC shifts right to u*'
- **SRPC₀:** Downward-sloping curve passing through (u*, π₀) at Time 0
- **SRPC₁:** Shifts UPWARD (adverse supply shock raises inflation at every unemployment level)

**What happens (Time 0 → Time 1):**
- The adverse supply shock shifts the SRPC upward.
- The economy moves from **Time 0** (on SRPC₀ at natural unemployment u*, inflation π₀) to **Time 1** on the new SRPC₁.
- At Time 1: **inflation is higher AND unemployment is higher** (stagflation on the Phillips curve diagram).

```
π
|          LRPC' LRPC
|            |    |
|    SRPC₁   |    |
|  /         |    |
π₁+----------+    |  (Time 1)
|    SRPC₀   |    |
π₀+----------|----|  (Time 0)
|         \  |    |
+------------|----+----> u
            u*  u*'
```

- Time 0: intersection of SRPC₀ with LRPC at (u*, π₀)
- Time 1: economy moves to a point on SRPC₁ above and to the right: higher inflation, higher unemployment

### Question 1.5 – Labour Market with Binding Minimum Wage (Time 0 → Time 1)

**Diagram description:**

- **Vertical axis:** Nominal Wage (W)
- **Horizontal axis:** Quantity of Labour (L)
- **Labour Supply (LS):** Upward-sloping
- **Labour Demand (LD₀):** Downward-sloping
- **W_min:** Horizontal line ABOVE equilibrium wage (binding minimum wage)
- At Time 0: Employment = L₀ (where W_min meets LD₀); unemployment gap = L_S - L₀

**Impact of breakdown (Time 0 → Time 1):**
- Higher electricity costs reduce firm profitability → firms reduce production → **labour demand shifts LEFT** (LD₀ → LD₁)
- The minimum wage remains in place (W_min is unchanged and legally binding)
- Workers cannot accept lower wages to restore employment
- Employment falls: **L₁ < L₀** (where W_min meets LD₁)
- Unemployment increases: the gap between labour supply (L_S) and labour demand (L₁) **widens**

```
W
|          LS
W_min ---+---+--------  (binding minimum wage)
|        |   |
|       L₁  L₀     LD₀
|            \      /
|             \ LD₁/
+-------------+--+----> L
              L₁ L₀  L_S
              ←gap→ (unemployment increases)
```

**Explanation:** With a binding minimum wage, wages cannot fall to the new equilibrium even though demand for labour has fallen. Firms hire fewer workers at the fixed W_min. This results in more involuntary unemployment (structural unemployment increases).

### Question 1.6.1 – Type of Policy

Given that the government wants to **keep economic activity high before elections**, and the economy has fallen into a recession (output below natural rate = deflationary gap), the government will conduct:

**→ Expansionary Fiscal Policy**

Specifically: **increasing government spending (G)** and/or **cutting taxes (T)**.

This is a discretionary fiscal policy aimed at shifting aggregate demand to the right to close the deflationary gap before the election.

### Question 1.6.2 – Impact on AD-AS Diagram (Time 2)

Expansionary fiscal policy increases aggregate demand:
- **AD shifts RIGHT** (AD₀ → AD₂), labelled **Time 2**
- Moving along SRAS₁, the new short-run equilibrium has:
  - **Higher output** (Y₂ > Y₁, partially recovering toward Y*')
  - **Higher price level** (P₂ > P₁)

The policy partially closes the deflationary gap but also further raises prices.

### Question 1.6.3 – IS-LM: Effectiveness of Fiscal Policy by Interest Sensitivity of Investment

Fiscal policy (increase in G) shifts the **IS curve to the right**.

The higher G increases income, which increases money demand, which raises the interest rate (i). A higher interest rate **crowds out** private investment. The effectiveness of fiscal policy depends on how strongly investment responds to this interest rate increase.

---

#### Diagram A – Interest Sensitivity of Investment is HIGH (Flat IS curve)

When investment is highly sensitive to interest rates, the IS curve is **flat**.

- A small rise in i causes a large fall in I → large crowding-out effect
- The rightward IS shift leads to only a **small increase in Y** (most of the stimulus is offset)
- Fiscal policy is **LESS effective**

```
i
|        LM
|       /
|      /    IS₁  IS₂
i₂ ---+----/------+--- (new eq.)
i₁ --/---+------------ (original eq.)
|   /   IS₁  IS₂
+--/---------------------> Y
      Y₁  Y₂
      (small ΔY)
```

The flat IS means crowding-out is high → small gain in Y.

---

#### Diagram B – Interest Sensitivity of Investment is LOW (Steep IS curve)

When investment barely responds to interest rates, the IS curve is **steep**.

- A large rise in i causes only a small fall in I → small crowding-out effect
- The rightward IS shift leads to a **large increase in Y**
- Fiscal policy is **MORE effective**

```
i
|        LM
|       /
|      /
i₂ ---+--------+------- (new eq.)
i₁ --/---+------------- (original eq.)
|   /   IS₁  IS₂
+--/---------------------> Y
        Y₁       Y₂
        (large ΔY)
```

**Conclusion:** Fiscal policy is most effective when the **interest sensitivity of investment is LOW** (steep IS curve), because the crowding-out effect is minimal.

### Question 1.7 – Long-Run Outcome (Time 3)

Since the breakdown is **permanent**, the long-run equilibrium must adjust:

**What happens:**
- In the long run, the SRAS continues to shift left/upward (wages and price expectations catch up with actual higher prices).
- The economy must settle at the **new (lower) natural rate of output Y*'** (because LRAS permanently shifted left in 1.2).
- The AD₂ (after government stimulus) intersects the new LRAS at **Time 3**:
  - **Output = Y*'** (lower than original Y*, but higher than Y₁)
  - **Price level = P₃** (significantly higher than P₀ due to both supply shock and stimulus)

**Add to diagram:** Draw a new SRAS₃ that intersects AD₂ and the new LRAS (Y*') at (Y*', P₃). This is labelled **Time 3**.

The long-run result is:
- **Permanently lower output** (structural loss from power station)
- **Significantly higher price level** (supply shock + fiscal stimulus)
- Government stimulus only succeeded in raising prices in the long run, not permanently raising output

### Question 2.1 – Money Market Diagram

**Answer: A – Both statements are correct**

**Statement 1 (A → D via QE):**
Quantitative easing = the central bank buys long-term financial assets from banks, injecting reserves → money supply (M) increases → the MS curve shifts RIGHT. In the diagram (value of money on vertical axis, nominal money on horizontal), the equilibrium moves to the right (more money) and downward (lower value of money = higher price level). If D is to the lower-right of A, this movement is correct. ✓

**Statement 2 (D → C via increased cash holding over demand deposits):**
When people shift holdings from demand deposits to cash, the currency-deposit ratio rises → the money multiplier decreases → the effective money supply (M1 = currency + demand deposits) contracts → MS shifts LEFT. The equilibrium moves to the left (less money) and upward (higher value of money). If C is to the upper-left of D, this movement is correct. ✓

Both statements are correct → **A**

### Question 2.3 – Before-Tax Nominal Interest Rate

**Answer: C – 15% < x ≤ 20%**

**Given:**
- After-tax real interest rate = 12%
- Inflation rate = 2%
- Tax rate = 12.5%

**Step 1:** After-tax nominal interest rate = after-tax real rate + inflation rate
= 12% + 2% = **14%**

**Step 2:** Tax is applied to the nominal interest rate:
After-tax nominal rate = x × (1 − tax rate)
14% = x × (1 − 0.125) = x × 0.875

**Step 3:** Solve for x:
x = 14% / 0.875 = **16%**

Since 15% < 16% ≤ 20% → **Answer: C**

### Question 2.4 – Trading Partner Growth

**Answer: C – Only Statement 2 is correct**

**Context:** Country X's trading partner has strong economic growth → partner's income rises → partner imports more from Country X → **Country X's exports increase → NX↑ → IS shifts right → AD increases**.

**Statement 1:** "Assume I = f(i, Y). The short-run effect of this event is theoretically ambiguous."

With I = f(i, Y):
- NX↑ → IS shifts right → in IS-LM, Y rises and i rises
- Higher Y → I increases (accelerator effect)
- Higher i → I decreases (standard effect)
- The effect on INVESTMENT (I) is ambiguous, but the effect on **total output (Y) is unambiguously positive**: the IS curve shifts right, LM is upward sloping → new intersection always gives a higher Y.

The stability condition of the Keynesian model requires ΔZ/ΔY < 1, ensuring a well-defined equilibrium. There is no ambiguity in the direction of Y. → Statement 1 is **INCORRECT**

**Statement 2:** "In the long run, the price level will increase more without automatic stabilizers."

With automatic stabilizers (e.g., progressive taxes, unemployment benefits):
- As income rises, taxes automatically increase and transfers decrease → dampens the AD increase → smaller rightward AD shift → smaller long-run price increase.

Without automatic stabilizers:
- The full multiplier effect plays out → larger AD shift → larger long-run price increase.

Since LRAS is vertical, a larger AD shift leads to a larger price level increase in the long run. → Statement 2 is **CORRECT**

→ **Answer: C**

### Question 2.5 – GDP Investment Component

**Answer: C – Dutch textile company sells t-1 carpet**

**Analysis of each option:**

**A) British firm builds new factory in London:**
- Building a new factory = Investment (I)
- BUT London is in the UK, which is **not part of the Eurozone**
- This does NOT affect Eurozone GDP → **WRONG**

**B) Belgian buys German car produced in year t:**
- Both Belgium and Germany are in the Eurozone → internal transaction, no NX effect
- A car purchased by a consumer = **Consumption (C)**, not Investment
- Does not affect I component → **WRONG**

**C) Dutch textile company sells carpet produced in year t-1:**
- The carpet was produced in t-1 → it was recorded as **inventory investment (+I)** in year t-1
- In year t, selling from inventory = **drawing down inventories → negative inventory investment (−I)** in year t
- Simultaneously increases C (or G, depending on buyer)
- This transaction **directly affects the Investment component (I)** of Eurozone GDP in year t → **CORRECT ✓**

**D) Greek resident buys an old house:**
- Old house = already produced in a past year, not new production
- Purchase of existing assets does not count in current-period GDP
- Does not affect any GDP component in year t → **WRONG**

→ **Answer: C**
